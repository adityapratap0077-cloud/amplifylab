Module 03

Learn the science

The thirty-second version of what the simulators above are doing.

🔥

Denaturation · 94–98 °C

Heat breaks the hydrogen bonds between base pairs and the double helix melts into two single strands — giving primers access to their binding sites.

🧲

Annealing · 50–65 °C

The reaction cools and primers latch onto their complementary sequences. Set this ~3–5 °C below primer Tm: too hot and nothing binds; too cold and primers stick everywhere.

🏗️

Extension · 72 °C

Taq polymerase extends each primer 5′ → 3′, copying the template. Each cycle doubles the target region — 30 cycles can turn one molecule into over a billion copies.

⚡

How gels separate DNA

DNA is negatively charged, so it migrates toward the positive electrode. The agarose matrix acts as a sieve: small fragments snake through quickly, large ones lag behind — separating purely by size.
